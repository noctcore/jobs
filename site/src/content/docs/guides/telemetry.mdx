## Metrics

`prometheusMetrics(promClient, { prefix?, registers?, buckets? })` registers:

| Metric | Type | Labels |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `jobs_job_duration_seconds` | histogram | `queue`, `job`, `ok` |
| `jobs_job_failures_total` | counter | `queue`, `job`, `final` |
| `jobs_sweep_failures_total` | counter | `sweep` |
| `jobs_sweep_last_success_timestamp_seconds` | gauge | `sweep` |

The default buckets reach five minutes, since jobs may take minutes. Alert on final failures and on
the age of a sweep's last success; a retried failure that later succeeded is not an incident.

## Tracing

`openTelemetryTracing(otel)` carries the producer's trace into the worker: `add()` injects the W3C
trace headers into the job's context envelope, and the worker extracts them and wraps each attempt
in a span. Exceptions are recorded on the span.

## Errors

`sentryReporter(Sentry)` calls `captureException` once per job when it fails for good (never on a
retry a later attempt may absorb) and on every failed sweep, with the queue, job name, job id and
attempt as tags and extras.

## Your own implementations

`JobsMetrics`, `JobsTracing` and `ErrorReporter` are small interfaces; every metrics method is
optional. Implement them to send the same signals anywhere else.

## Job context

A worker runs outside the request that enqueued its job, so the request id and similar fields are
lost unless they travel with the job. A `JobContextHook` carries them:

`capture()` runs on `add()`; its fields, with the trace headers, are stored under the reserved
`_jobs` key in the job data. In the worker the envelope is validated, removed before `handle` sees
the data, exposed as `job.context`, and `run()` re-enters it around the handler. It holds at most 32
string fields; put ids in it, never secrets. A malformed envelope coming back from Redis is dropped
whole.
